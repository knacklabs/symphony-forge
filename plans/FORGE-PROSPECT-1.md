# Research the prospect before a prototype and propose features from what is found

1 part · Risks: none one-way · New moving parts: none

## What changes for you

- The Sales person starts with the company's name and website, plus any notes or call transcripts they have. The agent researches the company itself. The Sales person does not need to know what to build. (Source: your decision, 11 Nov 2026)
- The agent does one research pass on the public web, with the web search of the session it already runs in. It looks at a fixed list: what the company sells and to whom, how it works today, complaints from its customers and its staff, the tools it uses, its job posts, and two or three close competitors. The pass stops after 12 searches and 20 pages read. The company itself gets up to 8 searches and 14 pages; the rest is kept for similar companies and is used only when little is found. These numbers are an estimate, not a measurement; we change them after the first real runs. There is no paid data service, and only one agent does the research. (Source: your decision, 11 Nov 2026; the numbers and the split are the planner's choice)
- The agent writes one page about the company, with a link for each finding, and keeps it with the client's other notes in the client's project files (the repo). It never goes into Forge's own files. It reads only public pages that open without a sign-in, names people only by their role, and writes short notes in its own words, with no copied text. (Source: your decision, 11 Nov 2026)
- The agent proposes one prototype: the one task the demo shows, and up to five features around it. Each feature has one line that says where it comes from. Two other possible prototypes follow, in a few lines each. The Sales person never picks from a long list of features. (Source: your decision, 11 Nov 2026)
- Each line has exactly one source mark, the same way in every case: a link to a page about this company; "from similar companies" with that company's name and a link; "heard from the customer" with the name of the note it comes from; "from the salesperson" for a feature the Sales person adds; or "not researched" for an idea with no source. "Not researched" appears only when the session cannot search the web or the pass did not reach that item. Nothing general appears as if it were about this company. (Source: your decision, 11 Nov 2026, "so nothing generic looks specific"; the last three marks are the planner's choice)
- When little is public about the company, the agent says plainly what it could not find. It asks the Sales person up to five questions about the company, studies two or three companies of the same kind and size, and marks each idea from them "from similar companies". A session that cannot search the web says so in one line and works from the notes and questions as today. (Source: your decision, 11 Nov 2026)
- The Sales person says yes, picks one of the other two, or says in plain words what to change. The agent writes the chosen prototype in full on the page and saves its task as the demo task in the product brief. The prototype's plan takes its demo steps from it. Nothing is built before this choice. (Source: your decision, 11 Nov 2026; where the choice is saved is the planner's choice)
- A second research pass or a second choice adds a new dated part to the page. It never removes the earlier findings or the earlier choice. (Source: the planner's choice)
- Before it shows the proposal, the agent checks that every line has its link or mark. The Sales person sees every link. Forge adds no check in code and no review rule for the page. (Source: the planner's choice; a review rule was cut after the plan's first read, because the review cannot see the first page in the normal case)
- The same page gives the five facts that the look of the app starts from: the industry, the users, the main job of the screens, brand words and brand files. The design step already reads the client's notes, so it reads this page with no change. (Source: your request, 11 Nov 2026)
- A new client repo gets these steps when it starts. An existing client repo gets the same steps when it moves to the new release and its guides are refreshed. No app file changes, and there is no new screen, command or setting. (Source: Forge's review rules)

Design: none (no screen; the Sales person sees the proposal in the chat)

## Why

When a Sales person asks the agent what the prototype should show, the agent gives a generic list for the industry. The Sales person is not a product person and cannot tell which of those features matter to this buyer, so the demo can miss the buyer's own problems. (Source: your words, 11 Nov 2026: "sales don't know what features to build for prototype and when they ask Agent, it's all going to be generic stuff all the time for the industry and I want agent to do the research and help sales person to decide on what to build")

## Done when

1. **From a company's name and website, plus any notes, the agent does one web research pass over a fixed list and stops at a fixed limit, with part of the limit kept for similar companies. It writes one page in the client repo with a link for each finding, and says plainly what it could not find. When little is public, it asks up to five questions and studies two or three similar companies. A session with no web search says so in one line.**
2. **The Sales person sees one proposed prototype: the one task the demo shows and up to five features, each with exactly one source mark, plus two other possible prototypes in a few lines each. The Sales person says yes, picks another or changes it in plain words, and the agent writes the chosen prototype in full and saves its task as the demo task before any build starts.**
3. **A second research pass or a second choice adds to the page and never removes the earlier findings or the earlier choice.**

## Risks

Risks: none one-way. The part changes guide text only, and reverts with its pull request. No data changes.

Trade-offs, in plain words:
- The page lives in the client's repo. If the client later gets access to that repo, they can read what the agent found about them, complaints included. The page keeps neutral words and links, and names no person.
- Nothing in Forge checks the marks after the agent writes them. The agent checks every line before it shows the proposal, and the Sales person sees every link. Nobody checks that a linked page really supports its finding.
- A fixed stop limit can end the pass before the list is done. The page then says which items were not reached, and ideas that depend on them are marked "not researched".

Model cost: unknown until the first real run, and not measured. The research runs once per prospect, in the session the Sales person already uses, on that session's model. A rough estimate is that 12 searches and 20 page reads add tens of thousands of words for the model to read. Forge adds no model call of its own.

### Settled (with the reason)

1. **The research page is the pre-meeting brief, grown.** One page, `docs/context/prospect-research.md`, in the client repo. The pre-meeting sentence and outline stay, and the new text says the brief is written as this page from one research pass. Run it before the first meeting, or before the prototype plan when the Sales person already met the prospect. Reason: no new document type, and `docs/context/` is where client notes live, so the journey, the brief and the design step read it with no change.
2. **Input.** The company's name and website, plus any notes or transcripts the Sales person gives, saved in `docs/context/` as today. When the session cannot read a recording, ask for its transcript. The agent asks nothing before the pass.
3. **The fixed list**, in this order: (1) what it sells and to whom, from its own site; (2) how it works today: its service or ordering pages, case studies and the duties in its job posts; (3) complaints from its customers and its staff, from public review pages and public employer review pages that open without sign-in; (4) its tools, from job posts, its site and vendors' public case studies; (5) its job posts, from its careers page and public job boards; (6) two or three close competitors and what each offers, from their own sites.
4. **The stop limit, split.** At most 12 web searches and 20 page reads for the whole pass. Items 1 to 6 use at most 8 searches and 14 page reads. The last 4 searches and 6 page reads are kept for similar companies and are used only when findings are thin (Settled 7). The page records what it used ("Company: 7 of 8 searches, 13 of 14 pages. Similar companies: 3 of 4 searches, 5 of 6 pages."). An item not reached is listed under `Not found` as "not reached". The guide says these numbers are an estimate to tune after the first real runs.
5. **One agent.** The session's own agent runs the pass. When the host offers a helper agent (for example the session's `explorer` role), the agent may hand the whole pass to one helper so the page reads stay out of the main chat. Never more than one.
6. **Rules for sources.** Public pages only, never a page behind a sign-in. No personal data about a named person: a person appears only by role ("the operations head"), and complaints are summarised with no reviewer names. Short notes in the agent's own words and a link; no copied text. Neutral words, because the client may one day read the repo. The guide holds this sentence word for word: "Every finding under What we found has its own link to the public page it comes from; a finding without a link is not written."
7. **Thin findings.** Fewer than three of items 1 to 5 hold a finding about this company. Then the agent (a) lists under `Not found` what it could not find and what it tried, (b) asks the Sales person up to five questions about the company in one message, under `Questions for you`, (c) studies two or three companies of the same kind and size with the kept part of the limit, and (d) marks every idea from them with the similar-company mark. It does not wait for the answers: it shows the proposal at once, and answers that arrive update it through a new dated part (Settled 11).
8. **No web search.** When the session has no web search or page reading, the agent says in one line: "This session cannot search the web, so I could not research <company>." It then works from the notes and questions as today. The marks follow Settled 9 unchanged.
9. **Source marks, one rule for every path.** Each line of a proposed feature, and each line of the two other prototypes, has exactly one mark, chosen by where the idea came from:
   - a link to a public page about this company;
   - `from similar companies: <company>` and a link to that company's page;
   - `heard from the customer: <note file in docs/context/>` (the existing transcript mark, with the source named);
   - `from the salesperson` (a feature the Sales person added in their reply);
   - `not researched` (an idea with no source), allowed only when the session cannot search the web or the pass did not reach the item the idea depends on.
   An idea with no source in any other case does not appear. So a transcript point in a session without web search is `heard from the customer: <file>`, and only the agent's own unsourced ideas are `not researched`. Before it shows the proposal, the agent checks every line against this list and fixes or removes a line with no mark. The guide holds the list word for word.
10. **The proposal.** Under `## Proposed prototype, <YYYY-MM-DD>`: the one task the demo shows, start to finish, then up to five features, one line each with its mark. Under `## Other possible prototypes, <YYYY-MM-DD>`: two numbered options, each a task and one or two lines with their marks. The agent shows the same text in the chat in plain words, with the links. The guessed problem cards on the page carry `Evidence:` with the finding and its mark; when the cards move into `docs/product/DISCOVERY.md`, the line keeps its mark and the `(guess)` mark until the customer confirms.
