# Research the prospect before a prototype and propose features from what is found

1 part · Risks: none one-way · New moving parts: none

## What changes for you

- The Sales person starts with the company's name and website, plus any notes or call transcripts they have. The agent researches the company itself. The Sales person does not need to know what to build. (Source: your decision, 11 Nov 2026)
- The agent does one research pass on the public web, with the web search of the session it already runs in. It looks at a fixed list: what the company sells and to whom, how it works today, complaints from its customers and its staff, the tools it uses, its job posts, and two or three close competitors. It stops after 12 searches and 20 pages read. These numbers are an estimate, not a measurement; we change them after the first real runs. There is no paid data service and only one agent does the research. (Source: your decision, 11 Nov 2026; the numbers are the planner's choice)
- The agent writes one page about the company, with a link for each finding, and keeps it with the client's other notes in the client's project files (the repo). It never goes into Forge's own files. It reads only public pages that open without a sign-in, names people only by their role, and writes short notes in its own words, with no copied text. (Source: your decision, 11 Nov 2026)
- The agent proposes one prototype: the one task the demo shows, and up to five features around it. Each feature has one line that says where it comes from: a finding about this company with its link, or one of the marks in the next point. Two other possible prototypes follow, in a few lines each. The Sales person never picks from a long list of features. (Source: your decision, 11 Nov 2026)
- When little is public about the company, the agent says plainly what it could not find. It asks the Sales person up to five questions about the company, studies two or three companies of the same kind and size, and marks each idea from them "from similar companies". An idea from the customer's own words is marked "heard from the customer", and a feature the Sales person adds is marked "from the salesperson". A session that cannot search the web says so in one line, works from the notes and questions as today, and marks its ideas "not researched". Nothing general appears as if it were about this company. (Source: your decision, 11 Nov 2026; the last three marks are the planner's choice)
- The Sales person says yes, picks one of the other two, or says in plain words what to change. The agent saves the choice as the demo task in the product brief, and the prototype's plan takes its demo steps from it. Nothing is built before this choice. (Source: your decision, 11 Nov 2026; where the choice is saved is the planner's choice)
- The same page gives the five facts that the look of the app starts from: the industry, the users, the main job of the screens, brand words and brand files. The design step already reads the client's notes, so it reads this page with no change. (Source: your request, 11 Nov 2026)
- The review of the change that saves the page reports a proposed feature that has no link and no mark. It does not stop the work; it goes on the Known issues list, the list of known problems that the board shows beside the item. (Source: the planner's choice, in place of a new check in code)
- A new client repo gets these steps when it starts. An existing client repo gets the same steps when it moves to the new release and its guides are refreshed. No app file changes, and there is no new screen, command or setting. (Source: Forge's review rules)

Design: none (no screen; the Sales person sees the proposal in the chat)

## Why

When a Sales person asks the agent what the prototype should show, the agent gives a generic list for the industry. The Sales person is not a product person and cannot tell which of those features matter to this buyer, so the demo can miss the buyer's own problems. (Source: your words, 11 Nov 2026: "sales don't know what features to build for prototype and when they ask Agent, it's all going to be generic stuff all the time for the industry and I want agent to do the research and help sales person to decide on what to build")

## Done when

1. **From a company's name and website, plus any notes, the agent does one web research pass over a fixed list and stops at a fixed limit. It writes one page in the client repo with a link for each finding, and says plainly what it could not find. When little is public, it asks up to five questions and studies two or three similar companies. A session with no web search says so in one line.**
2. **The Sales person sees one proposed prototype: the one task the demo shows and up to five features. Each feature has one line that names where it comes from: a finding about this company with its link, "from similar companies" with a link, the customer's or the Sales person's own words, or "not researched". Two other possible prototypes follow in a few lines each. The Sales person says yes, picks another or changes it in plain words, and the agent saves the choice as the demo task before any build starts.**
3. **The review of the change that saves the research page reports any proposed feature that has no such line.**

## Risks

Risks: none one-way. The part changes guide text and one review line, and reverts with its pull request. No data changes.

Trade-offs, in plain words:
- The page lives in the client's repo. If the client later gets access to that repo, they can read what the agent found about them, complaints included. The page keeps neutral words and links, and names no person.
- The review can see that a line has a link or a mark. It cannot see whether the linked page really supports the finding. The Sales person sees every link when the agent shows the proposal.
- A fixed stop limit can end the pass before the list is done. The page then says which items were not reached, and those ideas are marked "not researched".

Model cost: unknown until the first real run, and not measured. The research runs once per prospect, in the session the Sales person already uses, on that session's model. A rough estimate is that 12 searches and 20 page reads add tens of thousands of words for the model to read. Forge adds no model call of its own: the review line is part of the review that already runs on the change.

### Settled (with the reason)

1. **The research page is the pre-meeting brief, grown.** One page, `docs/context/prospect-research.md`, in the client repo. It replaces nothing: the pre-meeting sentence and outline stay, and the new text says the brief is written as this page from one research pass. Run it before the first meeting, or before the prototype plan when the Sales person already met the prospect. A second pass rewrites the page; git keeps the old one. Reason: no new document type, and `docs/context/` is where client notes live, so the journey, the brief and the design step read it with no change.
2. **Input.** The company's name and website, plus any notes or transcripts the Sales person gives, saved in `docs/context/` as today. When the session cannot read a recording, ask for its transcript. The agent asks nothing before the pass.
3. **The fixed list**, in this order: (1) what it sells and to whom, from its own site; (2) how it works today: its service or ordering pages, case studies and the duties in its job posts; (3) complaints from its customers and its staff, from public review pages and public employer review pages that open without sign-in; (4) its tools, from job posts, its site and vendors' public case studies; (5) its job posts, from its careers page and public job boards; (6) two or three close competitors and what each offers, from their own sites.
4. **The stop limit.** At most 12 web searches and 20 page reads for the whole pass, the similar-company study included. The page records what it used ("Searches: 9 of 12. Pages read: 17 of 20."). An item not reached is listed under `## Not found` as "not reached", and ideas that depend on it are marked "not researched". The guide says these numbers are an estimate to tune after the first real runs.
5. **One agent.** The session's own agent runs the pass. When the host offers a helper agent (for example the session's `explorer` role), the agent may hand the whole pass to one helper so the page reads stay out of the main chat. Never more than one.
6. **Rules for sources.** Public pages only, never a page behind a sign-in. No personal data about a named person: a person appears only by role ("the operations head"), and complaints are summarised with no reviewer names. Short notes in the agent's own words and a link; no copied text. Neutral words, because the client may one day read the repo.
7. **Thin findings.** Fewer than three of items 1 to 5 hold a finding about this company. Then the agent (a) lists under `## Not found` what it could not find and what it tried, (b) asks the Sales person up to five questions about the company in one message, under `## Questions for you`, (c) studies two or three companies of the same kind and size within the same limit, and (d) marks every idea from them `from similar companies: <company>` with a link. It does not wait for the answers: it shows the proposal at once, and answers that arrive update it.
8. **No web search.** When the session has no web search or page reading, the agent says in one line: "This session cannot search the web, so I could not research <company>." It then works from the notes and questions as today, and marks each idea `not researched`.
9. **Source marks.** Each line of a proposed feature, and each line of the two other prototypes, has exactly one of: a link to a page about this company; `from similar companies: <company>` with a link; `heard from the customer` with the note's file name (the existing transcript mark); `from the salesperson` (a feature the Sales person added); or `not researched`. A line with none of them is general and does not appear. Before it shows the proposal, the agent checks every line against this list.
10. **The proposal.** Under `## Proposed prototype`: the one task the demo shows, start to finish, then up to five features, one line each. Under `## Other possible prototypes`: two numbered options, each a task and one or two lines with their marks. The agent shows the same text in the chat in plain words, with the links. The guessed problem cards on the page carry `Evidence:` with the finding and its link or mark; when the cards move into `docs/product/DISCOVERY.md`, the line keeps its link and the `(guess)` mark until the customer confirms. The proposal is not the Options step: Options with payback stay for stories after sign-off.
11. **The choice is saved in two places that exist today.** The agent renames `## Proposed prototype` to `## Chosen prototype` and adds `- Chosen: yes | option <n> | changed: <their words> (salesperson, <YYYY-MM-DD>)`. It writes `- Demo workflow: <task> (salesperson, <YYYY-MM-DD>)` in the brief's Answers; the recap's client reply replaces it with `(client, <YYYY-MM-DD>)` as today. The prototype plan's demo steps come from the chosen task and features, the task first. All of this ships as one fix, as discovery notes do. Reason: the build reads the plan's demo steps and sign-off reads the brief's Answers already, so no new record is needed.
12. **The five facts.** The page has `## For the look`: industry, users, screen job, brand words, and brand files as links to the company's logo or brand page (no download). The design step reads the client's notes, so it reads this page. This story changes nothing in the design step.
13. **The review line.** In `review-light.md`, after the screen icon rule: "Also apply this research rule: in `docs/context/prospect-research.md`, report each line under `## Proposed prototype`, `## Chosen prototype` or `## Other possible prototypes` that has no link and none of `from similar companies`, `heard from the customer`, `from the salesperson` or `not researched`, as a finding titled `Research: <the feature>`." The last paragraph's "the three checks and the screen icon rule above" gains "and the research rule". It is not added to `review.md`: research happens before a prototype, so only the light review sees the page. It does not block (docs-only bar, see evidence).
14. **No worker brief change.** Workers do not research or choose; they build the plan's demo steps as today. The coordinator's guide (`skill.md`) and the light review carry the whole rule.
