# Codex build rounds run on a machine you own

2 parts · Risks: a Codex worker on your machine can read and change what the developer's user there can; turning it off deletes Forge's folder there · New moving parts: your own Linux machine, reached with ssh; one small shared setting and record for work on other machines

## What changes for you

In this plan, "your machine" is a Linux computer that your team owns and that a developer can reach with `ssh` from the laptop.

- A Codex build round can run on your machine instead of on the laptop. The laptop sends the code to your machine over ssh, Codex works there, and the commits come back to the laptop. The laptop then pushes to GitHub, as today. (Source: your decisions of 17 Nov 2026)
- Your machine goes first, because it costs nothing more. A round starts there while its load is under 1.5 per core and it has a free place (half its processors), the same rule that the laptop uses. Otherwise the round runs on the laptop. (Source: your decisions of 17 Nov 2026)
- Only Codex build rounds go to your machine. Claude rounds, reviews, cold reads and the tests that close runs stay on the laptop. Claude has one way to run on your own machine with no person at the screen, and it needs a Team or Enterprise plan, a switch by the plan's owner, and a feature that is still in beta. Codex rounds that a Codex session runs as its own helpers also stay on the laptop. (Source: your decision of 17 Nov 2026)
- The worker's own test runs move with its round: they run on your machine, with its processors. (Source: your decision of 17 Nov 2026)
- It is off in every repo. The agent turns it on only after you agree, in a small settings change: `boxes = "ssh"` in `forge.toml`. This is the same setting that the cloud machine story uses; a repo uses one of the two. Each developer then names their own machine in their `forge.local.toml` (`host = "<the name they give ssh>"`), which git does not share. A new client repo has it off at setup. An existing client repo also has it off after it upgrades and syncs; its settings and its work do not change. Both get the new steps in Forge's guide. (Source: your decisions of 17 Nov 2026)
- One machine per developer in this first version. (Source: your decision of 17 Nov 2026)
- Setup happens once for each copy of the repo on the laptop, with one Forge step that the agent runs. It checks that ssh reaches the machine without a password, and that the machine has git, Forge at this repo's version and a Codex sign-in. It installs the Codex kit that Forge drives (about 300 MB) when it is missing, and it makes Forge's own folder there. When something is missing, it says what in one line, with the next step. It is safe to run again. (Source: your decisions of 17 Nov 2026)
- The developer signs in to Codex on the machine once, with their own account (`codex login --device-auth`). Forge never copies or reads a sign-in. On a shared machine, each developer uses their own user. (Source: your decision of 17 Nov 2026)
- Codex on the machine must trust Forge's folder there, because Codex runs Forge's safety checks only in a folder it trusts. Setup prints the two lines to add to Codex's settings on the machine, once. (Source: the planner's choice; confirm)
- Your machine needs no GitHub sign-in. Code goes to it straight over ssh, and commits come back the same way. (Source: your decisions of 17 Nov 2026)
- Each round starts in a new folder on your machine, and Forge deletes the folder once the commits are back. Rounds share the machine, as they share the laptop. When the repo's test command installs packages (for example `npm ci`), each new folder installs them again. (Source: your decision of 17 Nov 2026; the install cost is the planner's note)
- Only committed files reach your machine. A repo whose tests need an ignored settings file (such as `.env.test`), or a service that the machine does not have, keeps this off for now. (Source: the planner's choice; confirm)
- The machine view and the Forge pane show the round as an ordinary row with the words "on <machine name>". It takes none of the laptop's places. `forge stop` ends it, as it ends a laptop round. (Source: your decisions of 17 Nov 2026)
- When your machine is busy, not set up or cannot be reached, the round runs on the laptop, and Forge says why in one line. (Source: your decisions of 17 Nov 2026)
- When the laptop sleeps or the connection breaks, the round ends. Its commits stay on your machine, and the next round of that item brings them back first. Changes that the worker had not committed are lost. (Source: your decision of 17 Nov 2026; the part about changes not committed is the planner's note)
- A Codex conversation lives on the machine where it ran. When an item's rounds move between the laptop and your machine, the next round starts a new conversation with the whole brief, which uses more tokens than a continued one. (Source: the planner's note)
- To turn it off, a settings change sets `boxes = "off"`, and each developer has their agent run the turn-off step. That step deletes Forge's folder on the machine. Commits that had not come back are kept on the laptop first. (Source: your decision of 17 Nov 2026)
- Cost: no new model cost and nothing to pay. Codex on your machine uses the developer's own Codex account, as on the laptop. How much sooner work gets ready is not measured yet. (Source: your decision of 17 Nov 2026 that your machine goes first because it is free)
- Forge's own repo builds with Claude, so Forge's tests use a stand-in for the machine on the test computer. Before the release, Forge's team tries it once with a test repo that builds with Codex and a real Linux machine. (Source: the planner's choice)

Design: none (no screen: setup and turn-off print lines in the agent's terminal, and the machine view and the pane gain the words "on <machine name>").

## Why

Every Codex build round runs on the laptop. A new round waits while the laptop's load is at 1.5 per core or more, or while all its places are taken, and the team's own Linux machines sit idle. A client team waits for this. Today's workaround: the agent starts fewer rounds at once, and the rest wait. In Forge's own repo, build rounds took about 8% of item time (measured over 86 items, 11 to 14 Oct 2026); how long the client team's rounds wait is not measured.

## Done when

1. **A developer sets up their own Linux machine for a copy of the repo once: Forge checks it, installs what it can, and says in one line what is missing and what to do next.**
2. **With the setting on, a Codex build round starts on that machine while it has room, its log shows on the laptop as it runs, and its commits come back to the laptop's branch.**
3. **The machine view and the pane show such a round as an ordinary row marked with the machine's name, it takes none of the laptop's places, and stop ends it.**
4. **When the machine is busy, not set up or cannot be reached, the round runs on the laptop and Forge says why in one line, and commits that a cut-off round left on the machine come back before the item's next round.**
5. **Rounds on a machine you own are off in every repo until a settings change turns them on, and turning them off deletes Forge's folder on the machine after it keeps any commits that had not come back.**

## Risks

These steps are sensitive or cannot be undone:

- Forge runs Codex with full access on your machine, as it does on the laptop. A worker there can read and change anything that the developer's user can: other repos, keys and files. Use a user that holds only what the work needs.
- Turning it off deletes Forge's folder on the machine. Changes that a worker had not committed are lost then, and also when a round is cut off. Commits are kept on the laptop first.
- Codex's trust entry for Forge's folder stays in Codex's settings on the machine after turn-off; the developer removes it there if they want.

Accepted limits, in plain words:
- Codex build rounds only. Claude rounds stay on the laptop until Claude's own way is out of beta and on the team's plan.
- A sleeping laptop or a broken connection ends the round; its commits wait on the machine until the item's next round.
- One machine per developer.
- Close runs its tests on the laptop. A pass that the worker's tests recorded on your machine does not count there, because each machine keeps its own record of passed tests.
- A conversation that ran on your machine is not linked to the pull request in the Codex app on the laptop.

### The test machine

`tests/ssh_host.py`, a helper module (not a test file), shared by both parts. `machine(repo, name="devbox", **facts)` puts a program named `ssh` first on the test `PATH` and makes `tmp_path/hosts/<name>/home`. An unknown host, or a `down` file in the host folder, exits 255 with `ssh: Could not resolve hostname <name>`; a `fail-<word>` file makes the next call whose command holds that word (`git-upload-pack`, `git-receive-pack`) exit 255 once.
